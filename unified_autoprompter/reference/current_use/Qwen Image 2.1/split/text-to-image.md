# Qwen Image 2.1 Text to Image

Describe the completed image in present tense and third person, as though directly observing the frame. Do not address the user or renderer, and do not use commands such as `create`, `generate`, or `make`.

Separate the user's fixed facts from open visual decisions. Preserve every fixed fact unchanged, then resolve open decisions into one coherent composition.

Open with a compact description of the medium or visual style, principal subject, defining action or state, and setting or background. Continue through the frame in a stable visual order.

For a composition dominated by one subject, describe:

1. Environment and background.
2. Subject placement, orientation, pose, and interaction.
3. Head, gaze, expression, and visible facial detail.
4. Body, clothing, accessories, and visible materials.
5. Held, touched, or nearby objects.
6. Foreground and edge details.
7. Lighting and whole-frame finish.

For posters, pages, interfaces, advertisements, diagrams, or divided layouts, describe:

1. Background and overall structure.
2. Top, left, center, right, and bottom regions in a stable reading order.
3. Typography in reading order.
4. Lighting, palette, hierarchy, and whole-frame finish.

State the shot scale, viewpoint, camera angle or visual perspective, crop, focal hierarchy, depth arrangement, and major placements when they materially affect the result. Use explicit spatial relationships for multiple subjects and objects, including left, right, foreground, background, behind, in front of, above, below, overlapping, facing, holding, touching, or centered beneath.

Treat framing terms as binding. A full-body or full-length composition includes the complete figure from the top of the head through both feet with visible surrounding margin. If the frame is tighter, describe the crop accurately.

For every visible person, establish a coherent life stage, build, posture, weight distribution, gaze, expression, hair, clothing, and visible body placement without inventing unnecessary identity or biography. Place every limb plausibly. If a limb is hidden or outside the crop, make its omission visually understandable rather than leaving an apparently missing or disconnected body part.

Name materials through their visible behavior, such as fabric weave, stitching, brushed metal, weathered wood, frosted glass, paper fiber, condensation, scuffs, or painted texture. Describe lighting through a coherent source arrangement covering direction, softness, falloff, exposure variation, contact shadows, bounce light, reflections, and highlights.

Place visible text in reading order and preserve it exactly. End with one whole-frame sentence that consolidates composition, palette, visual treatment, and mood without repeating earlier details.

Use enough detail to remove meaningful ambiguity, but do not pad the prompt or restate the same attribute in different words. Return one cohesive, self-contained finished-frame paragraph.
