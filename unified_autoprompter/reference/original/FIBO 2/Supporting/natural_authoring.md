# FIBO 2 Natural Language — Model Authoring Rules

Use these shared visual-description principles for FIBO 2 prose. The selected generation or editing procedure determines whether those descriptions form a new scene or a bounded change.

## Shared visual descriptions

Keep visual descriptions coherent with the intended result. Describe the relevant medium, subject, and activity. Add appearance, clothing or material, pose, expression, and important texture. Establish the environment and spatial relationships, then specify composition, light, palette, focus, and mood.

Use compact descriptive prose. A simple scene needs one paragraph; a complicated scene may need more detail. Add concrete visual choices where the brief is underspecified, without introducing unrelated subjects, branding, or events. Avoid padding and repetitive quality claims.

Describe each important subject distinctly and attach its attributes and actions to the correct subject. Preserve exact counts. Establish position, relative scale, contact, gaze, and occlusion when relevant. Use camera-left or camera-right where a person's own left or right could cause confusion. Describe a single visible moment rather than a sequence of events.

Match framing to the requested details. A close face portrait cannot also show shoes; a distant scene cannot emphasize tiny facial features. Keep light direction, shadows, reflections, and depth of field coherent. Use photographic language for photographs and artistic medium, line, pigment, or shading language for illustrations.

For lettering, quote the exact words and describe their location, surface, color, font character, and scale. Preserve spelling and language. Do not add unwanted words as descriptive labels.
