# WAN 2.2 Video to Video

## Generation type

- **WAN Animate:** use a character image and motion or performance source for the selected animation, replacement, or reenactment task.

### Action and temporal development

Turn a static idea into a causal movement sequence. Establish the opening state, principal action, intermediate change when needed, and usable final state. Keep motion physically plausible and proportionate to clip length. Do not overload a short clip with unrelated actions.

## WAN Animate method

Separate appearance authority from motion authority.

- The character image controls the target character’s identity and visible appearance.
- The performer video controls body movement and facial performance through temporally aligned motion guidance. Its character identity is not inherited.
- Do not import the source performer’s identity, wardrobe, background, or incidental objects unless requested.
- Preserve subject count, body coherence, contact points, and scene placement through large motion.
- For character animation, generate the referenced character performing the driving video’s expression and movement without importing the driving scene.
- For character replacement, use the driving video as the scene and temporal master, replace its performer with the referenced character, preserve expression and motion, and reproduce the source lighting and color tone so the new character integrates into the environment.
- Preserve occlusion, contact, scale, spatial placement, camera motion, and temporal alignment from the driving scene when replacement requires them.
- Relighting is part of replacement coherence: describe the target character under the source environment’s direction, intensity, color, and shadow logic rather than as a separately lit cutout.
- Describe the final performance and scene as a coherent video, not as a list of source files.

## Timing and shot logic

Use one continuous shot when camera presentation and principal action remain materially continuous. Introduce a new shot or timed beat when the camera materially changes or the principal action changes enough to require a separate phase. If duration is known, distribute action and dialogue realistically across it. If duration is absent, describe chronological phases without inventing frame-accurate timing.

Every cut should reveal a new subject, state, viewpoint, space, or time. Prefer camera motion over a cut when only distance or angle changes.
